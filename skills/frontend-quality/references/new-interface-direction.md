# New interface direction

Read for a new product surface without an established visual identity, or an explicitly requested redesign. Skip this exploration for a small edit or a brief that already specifies the composition. Respect supplied references and art direction.

## Choose before implementing

Use the brief to identify the dominant activity, the relationships in its content, and the intended experience: sustained dense work, quick decisions, exploration, reading, or another concrete use. Missing brand guidance permits ordinary visual choices; it does not require another approval.

Consider two or three plausible compositions briefly before coding. They must differ in information hierarchy, spatial organization, or interaction structure, not merely palette, border radius, or sidebar placement. Compare how each supports the primary task and what it makes harder. These are alternatives to reason about, not a requirement to build multiple prototypes or present a design report.

Choose one using the actual content and workflow. Do not select novelty for its own sake, borrow a previous project's shell by habit, or treat a familiar dashboard as the default for every application. When the brief already determines the structure, develop it rather than manufacturing alternatives.

## Make a coherent visual choice

Define the choices that make the selected composition tangible:

- **Typography:** roles, relative scale, weight, reading measure, and treatment of data versus prose. Choose for content and audience; a new font dependency is not necessary.
- **Composition:** the visual starting point, dominant region, supporting regions, alignment, and task/action relationship. Explain why their proportions suit the work.
- **Density and rhythm:** what deserves proximity or breathing room, and how frequently users scan, compare, or enter content. Neither maximum compactness nor large empty bands is a default.
- **Color and surfaces:** the roles of contrast, accent, boundaries, imagery, and depth. Expressive choices are allowed when coherent and usable; neutral styling is not inherently more correct.

Record a short rationale in working notes so implementation follows one direction. Do not put this rationale into interface captions. Use accessible ready-made controls for behavior; adapt their presentation to the chosen system instead of inheriting the library's entire demo layout.

## Assess the result

Inspect whether typography, proportions, density, and emphasis actually express the chosen direction in the rendered page. If they do not, correct the composition rather than adding decorative text or recoloring a generic shell. Retain deliberate identity during distillation; remove redundant content and unusable ornament, not every expressive choice.

Preserve meaningful labels, truthful states, keyboard behavior, responsive usability, and the existing prohibition on empty subtitles and eyebrows. Passing those checks establishes baseline quality, not that the design is compelling. Distinguish observed usability defects from aesthetic judgments when reporting results.
